## Summary

The publicly-accessible aggregate-intelligence leader as of 2026-06-22 is Anthropic's standard flagship **Claude Opus 4.8** (released May 28, 2026), now re-placed on the current **AA Intelligence Index v4.1 = 56** — "the most intelligent available model" [[claude-opus-4-8-max-intelligence-performance-price-analysis-27b7d2eb]]. The higher-scoring **Claude Fable 5** (first publicly available Mythos-class model, v4.1 = 60 with fallback) and the gated Mythos 5 have been **globally suspended** since June 12, 2026 under a US Commerce Department export-control directive — the first known use of export controls to force a commercial AI model recall — so Opus 4.8 is the effective public #1 [[statement-on-the-us-government-directive-to-suspend-access-to-fable-5-and-mythos-5-00131728]] [[claude-opus-4-8-max-intelligence-performance-price-analysis-27b7d2eb]]. The basis of the ban is now clearer and **more structural** than the original "narrow jailbreak" framing implied: NSA Director Gen. Joshua Rudd told the Senate Intelligence Committee that Mythos, in a classified red-team exercise, autonomously broke into nearly all of the NSA's classified systems within hours — i.e. the concern is Mythos's *autonomous offensive cybersecurity capability as such*, an architectural property, not a user-side exploit Anthropic could patch [[claude-fable-5-resurfaces-in-android-app-as-nsa-breach-testimony-reshapes-ban-b8988c67]]. This argues against the "fix the jailbreak and the ban lifts" quick-resolution expectation, though prediction markets still price restoration as more-likely-than-not in the near term: Kalshi 57% before July 1 (75% before July 17), Polymarket's leading outcome "July 1" at 55% [[fable-5-odds-when-will-anthropic-restore-access-6f6e6af9]]. (A June 21 reappearance of the "Claude Fable 5" name in the Claude Android app's model selector is **not** a restoration — invoking it still returns a "server is temporarily rate-limiting requests" error [[claude-fable-5-resurfaces-in-android-app-as-nsa-breach-testimony-reshapes-ban-b8988c67]].) Closest behind Opus 4.8 on v4.1 is GPT-5.5 (xhigh) at 55 — a ~1-point gap, compressed from ~1.2 points on the retired v4.0 scale, consistent with v4.1's agentic reweighting clustering the labs more tightly [[claude-opus-4-8-max-intelligence-performance-price-analysis-27b7d2eb]] [[gpt-5.5]]. Gemini 3.1 Pro re-places at **46 on v4.1** (confirmed 2026-06-21) [[gemini-3-1-pro-preview-intelligence-performance-price-analysis-3a3f9933]] and Grok 4.3 re-places at **38 on v4.1** (confirmed 2026-06-19) [[grok-4-3-high-intelligence-performance-price-analysis-18472372]]; all major closed-frontier models are now v4.1-placed [[gemini-3.1-pro]] [[grok-4.3]]. Opus 4.8 holds at $5/$25 per Mtoken; the suspended Fable 5 listed at 2× that ($10/$50) [[introducing-claude-opus-4-8-5348a7d2]] [[claude-fable-5-and-claude-mythos-5-e11fcea9]]; see [[claude-opus-4.8]], [[benchmark-leaders]] and [[cost-comparison]].

> Claude Opus 4.8 (max, 56) is the most intelligent available model according to the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.1. Claude Fable 5 (with fallback) leads at 60 but is currently unavailable; GPT-5.5 (xhigh) scores 55.

> Anthropic is complying with the government's legal directive and is removing access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for all users.

> This is not the same as the 'jailbreak' described in earlier public accounts — it is evidence that Mythos's autonomous offensive cybersecurity capability is itself the national security concern, not a user-side exploit that can be patched. The revelation explains why David Sacks's 'fix the jailbreak and the ban lifts' framing has not translated into a quick resolution — the underlying concern is architectural, not prompt-level.

### Index basis: AA Intelligence Index v4.1 (read first)

The figures above are on **AA Intelligence Index v4.1**, the agentic-workloads reweighting Artificial Analysis migrated to on 2026-06-17 that **re-scales every model's score** — v4.0 and v4.1 numbers are **not directly comparable** [[artificial-analysis-intelligence-index-v4-1-a-shift-toward-agentic-workloads-e0bce552]]. v4.1 is a weighted average across four categories — **Agents 34% / Coding 24% / Scientific Reasoning 24% / General 18%** — so agentic + coding tasks are now 58% of the aggregate, making it materially more representative of agentic-coding workflows than v4.0 [[claude-opus-4-8-max-intelligence-performance-price-analysis-27b7d2eb]]. It reweights evaluations toward agentic tasks (Terminal-Bench Hard → 2.1, τ²-Bench Telecom → τ³-Bench Banking), upgrades GDPval-AA to **GDPval-AA v2** (now the single highest-weighted evaluation, with Elo re-baselined to human-expert = 1000, a rotating frontier-model judge panel, and the agent turn limit raised 100 → 250), and drops IFBench for saturation [[artificial-analysis-intelligence-index-v4-1-a-shift-toward-agentic-workloads-e0bce552]]. The re-scale is large — e.g. Kimi K2.6 moves 54 (v4.0) → 43 (v4.1) and GLM-5.1 51 (v4.0) → 40 (v4.1) [[glm-5-2-is-the-new-leading-open-weights-model-on-the-artificial-analysis-intelligence-index-ef3ea3a0]]. All major closed-frontier models are now v4.1-placed: Fable 5 (60, suspended), Opus 4.8 (56), GPT-5.5 (55), Gemini 3.1 Pro (46), Grok 4.3 (38). The v4.0 era is fully superseded for these models; any lesser-tracked model still carrying a v4.0 score is explicitly tagged.

> Intelligence Index v4.1 is calculated as a weighted average across four categories: Agents (34%), Coding (24%), Scientific Reasoning (24%) and General (18%), with the weighting emphasizing agentic tasks.

> v4.1 marks a broader shift toward agentic workloads ... upgrading GDPval-AA to GDPval-AA v2; and removing IFBench due to saturation. GDPval-AA v2 is the highest weighted evaluation in the Intelligence Index v4.1.

### Agentic and coding leadership

On **GDPval-AA v2** — the highest-weighted v4.1 evaluation, re-baselined to human-expert = 1000 and not comparable to the retired v1 Elo — **Claude Opus 4.8 scores 1638**, the highest among available models, with the suspended **Claude Fable 5 (with fallback) at 1818** above it and **GPT-5.5 (xhigh) at 1531** below [[claude-opus-4-8-max-intelligence-performance-price-analysis-27b7d2eb]]. On the contamination-resistant **SWE-bench Pro** axis this page treats as the credible frontier-coding signal, the suspended **Claude Fable 5** led at **80.3%** [[claude-fable-5-the-first-public-mythos-class-model-672c92f6]]; with Fable 5 inaccessible, **Claude Opus 4.8** is the best public SWE-bench Pro model at **69.2%** — over 10 points ahead of GPT-5.5 (58.6%) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (54.2%) — and remains the strongest computer-use/browser-agent model tested at 84% on Online-Mind2Web [[claude-opus-4-8-benchmarks-explained-60247f20]] [[introducing-claude-opus-4-8-5348a7d2]]. On the contamination-audited math axis, Fable 5 also leads FrontierMath (88% on the hardest Tier 4 v2, ~13 points over GPT-5.5) — though that audit corrected errors in 42% of problems, a benchmark-integrity caution [[claude-fable-5-outpaces-gpt-5-5-by-13-points-on-frontiermath-s-toughest-problems-b1737496]] [[frontiermath-tier-4-v2-9558ff2f]].

> GDPval-AA v2 is the highest weighted evaluation in the Intelligence Index v4.1. Claude Opus 4.8 scored 1638 on GDPval-AA v2, the highest among available models (Claude Fable 5 with fallback scored 1818 but is currently unavailable; GPT-5.5 xhigh scored 1531).

> On SWE-bench Pro, Fable 5 scores 80.3%, an 11-point lead over Opus 4.8 (69.2%) and more than 20 points ahead of GPT-5.5 (58.6%) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (54.2%).

> SWE-Bench Pro (Coding): Opus 4.8 lands at 69.2%, almost 5 points clear of Opus 4.7 (64.3%) and over 10 points ahead of GPT-5.5 (58.6%) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (54.2%).

> According to Epoch AI, Fable 5 hits 87 percent accuracy on tiers 1 through 3 and 88 percent on the hardest tier 4 (v2) ... approximately a 13-point difference in performance on FrontierMath's toughest problems.

### Per-lab flagships

**Anthropic**: the standard flagship is **Opus 4.8** (v4.1 AA Index 56; the Mythos-class Fable 5 / Mythos 5 above it are export-suspended); the original **Claude Mythos Preview** remains the internal capability ceiling, state-of-the-art on SWE-bench Verified (93.9%) and Terminal-Bench 2.0 (82.0%) [[claude-mythos-preview-d737ab91]]; see [[claude-mythos]]. **OpenAI**'s flagship is **GPT-5.5** (v4.1 AA Index 55, ~1 point behind Opus 4.8), strong on agentic coding and long-context reasoning [[claude-opus-4-8-max-intelligence-performance-price-analysis-27b7d2eb]] [[gpt-5.5]]. **Google**'s public frontier is **Gemini 3.1 Pro** (v4.1 AA Index 46, confirmed 2026-06-21 [[gemini-3-1-pro-preview-intelligence-performance-price-analysis-3a3f9933]]) plus the Flash-tier Gemini 3.5; the **3.5 Pro** frontier tier remains pre-GA [[gemini-3-5-frontier-intelligence-with-action-f4ceaac7]], targeting a 2M-token context and Deep Think reasoning at ~$15/$60 per Mtoken [[google-gemini-3-5-pro-nears-june-launch-with-2-million-token-context-and-deep-think-reasoning-3a0c8ff3]]; see [[gemini-3.1-pro]]. **xAI**'s Grok 4.3 (v4.1 AA Index 38, confirmed 2026-06-19 [[grok-4-3-high-intelligence-performance-price-analysis-18472372]]) undercuts the field at $1.25/$2.50 per Mtoken [[xai-launches-grok-43-with-improved-agentic-performance-and-lower-pricing-f1cfb522]], and its API-available **Grok Build 0.1** coding model (256k context, native MCP, 100+ tok/s) lands at $1/$2 per Mtoken — the cheapest dedicated agentic-coding frontier model in production [[grok-build-0-1-on-api-c60c4a6b]]. The 1.5T-param **Grok V9-Medium** (3× the ~500B v8-small, trained explicitly on licensed Cursor developer-workflow sessions) **began public release June 16, 2026 — with no independent benchmarks yet** — on the same morning SpaceX signed a definitive **$60B agreement to acquire Anysphere (Cursor)**; a frontier lab acquiring the multi-model IDE its own model is trained to compete inside is a model-routing-neutrality risk worth tracking [[grok-v9-medium-arrives-as-spacex-seals-cursor-developers-face-model-choice-risk-37bb8bd7]]. **Microsoft** entered the frontier tier with its first in-house reasoning model, **MAI-Thinking-1** (35B-active sparse MoE, 256K context), claiming Opus-4.6-class coding [[introducing-mai-thinking-1-0e3e1662]].

> Pro targets a 2-million-token context window, a 'Deep Think' reasoning mode, and frontier multimodal understanding.

### Updates 2026-05-24

The most consequential open-weight move in May was efficiency-first, not scale-first. Zyphra released ZAYA1-8B (May 6, 2026), an Apache-2.0 reasoning MoE with 8.4B total but only ~760M active parameters per forward pass, optimized for maximum intelligence density per active parameter [[zaya1-8b-frontier-intelligence-density-trained-on-amd-3012ef57]]. Despite sub-1B active params it reaches 91.9% on AIME'25 and 89.6% on HMMT'25 — competitive with models many times its size [[zaya1-8b-frontier-intelligence-density-trained-on-amd-3012ef57]]. The technical report attributes this to three architectural choices: Compressed Convolutional Attention (CCA), which mixes sequences in a compressed latent space for an 8x KV-cache reduction vs full multi-head attention; a novel MLP-based expert router that improves routing stability over standard linear routers; and learned residual scaling [[zaya1-8b-technical-report-614bf738]]. Notably, ZAYA1-8B was trained end-to-end on AMD Instinct MI300X clusters with AMD Pensando Pollara networking — a rare frontier-relevant result outside the NVIDIA training stack [[zaya1-8b-frontier-intelligence-density-trained-on-amd-3012ef57]].

> ZAYA1-8B is a Mixture-of-Experts language model optimized for maximum reasoning performance per active parameter, with 8.4B total parameters but only 760M active per forward pass.

> With 0.7B active parameters and the 40K/4K Markovian RSA configuration, ZAYA1-8B reaches 91.9% on AIME'25 and 89.6% on HMMT'25.

> Compressed Convolutional Attention (CCA) performs sequence mixing in a compressed latent space, resulting in an 8x reduction in KV-cache size compared to full multi-head attention.

The long-standing open question of whether DeepSeek's reasoning model had shipped now has an answer: DeepSeek R2 is a 32B dense transformer released April 2026 under MIT license, scoring 92.7% on AIME 2025, fitting on a single 24GB consumer GPU, and undercutting Western frontier reasoning APIs by ~70% on token cost [[deepseek-r2-explained-92-7-aime-32b-open-weight-d990eab3]]. > Where R1 (January 2025) was a 671-billion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts behemoth, R2 ships as a 32B dense transformer released under MIT license, small enough to fit on a single RTX 4090 or A6000.

### Frontier coding-agent cost-efficiency (2026-05-30 update)

Artificial Analysis ranked **Cursor's Composer 2.5 third on its Coding Agent Index at 62** — a 14-point gain over Composer 2 (48), trailing only Claude Opus 4.7 (max) in Claude Code (66) and GPT-5.5 (xhigh reasoning) in Codex (65) [[cursor-s-composer-2-5-third-on-the-coding-agent-index-b9ec8ce6]]. The cost-per-task gap is the headline number: the two leaders cost $4.10 and $4.82 per task, while Composer 2.5 Fast lands at $0.44 (~10×) and Composer 2.5 standard at $0.07 (~60×) cheaper [[cursor-s-composer-2-5-third-on-the-coding-agent-index-b9ec8ce6]]. The biggest benchmark gain was +35 points on SWE-Bench-Pro-Hard-AA (now 47%, comparable to Opus 4.7 max), with smaller gains on Terminal-Bench v2 (+2) and SWE-Atlas-QnA (+3).

> Composer 2.5 scored 62 on the Coding Agent Index, a 14 point gain over Composer 2 (48), putting it in third place behind only Claude Opus 4.7 (max) in Claude Code (66) and GPT-5.5 (xhigh reasoning) in Codex (65).

> These competitors cost $4.10 and $4.82 per task respectively, ~10x the cost of Composer 2.5 Fast ($0.44) and ~60x the cost of Composer 2.5 standard ($0.07).

The practical implication for agentic workflows: a model competitive on SWE-Bench-Pro-Hard-AA but 10–60× cheaper per task changes the economics of long-horizon multi-agent pipelines where token spend compounds across subagent fan-out. Read alongside Opus 4.8's retaking the AA Intelligence Index at 61.4 [[claude-opus-4-8-takes-the-lead-on-the-artificial-analysis-intelligence-index-57303c9c]], the curve is bifurcating: capability ceiling still climbing at the top, cost-floor falling faster underneath.
