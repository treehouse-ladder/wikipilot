## Summary

The first daily research pass surfaces a 2026 dominated by retrospective vinyl releases [[chrono-trigger-original-soundtrack-vinyl-box-to-release-in-april-2026-f0889eea]] and the maturation of adaptive-audio middleware choices for Unreal Engine 5 [[wwise-vs-fmod-vs-metasounds-choosing-audio-middleware-for-your-ue5-game-in-2026-9afdff3e]].

**Vinyl releases.** Square Enix is shipping a 30th-anniversary 4xLP box set of Yasunori Mitsuda's Chrono Trigger Original Soundtrack on March 25, 2026 in Japan and April 2026 in North America, with 64 tracks, Akira Toriyama jacket art, and a commemorative message from Mitsuda inscribed on the box lid [[chrono-trigger-original-soundtrack-vinyl-box-to-release-in-april-2026-f0889eea]]. Bandai Namco is shipping a 7LP Elden Ring: Nightreign vinyl collection in summer 2026, capturing 57 tracks across the Limveld map and the Forsaken Hollows DLC in a limited run of 4,999 copies [[elden-ring-nightreign-soundtrack-is-coming-to-vinyl-30e2c324]]. Capcom's Pragmata arrived in April 2026 with a 46-track score by Yasumasa Kitagawa blending orchestral emotion, ambient piano, and synth-driven tension, with YU-KA performing the ending theme "Memories Are You" [[pragmata-soundtrack-and-vinyl-release-review-2026-31dc604e]].

> To commemorate the 30th anniversary of Chrono Trigger, Square Enix will release a vinyl box set of the game's original soundtrack on March 25, 2026 in Japan and April 2026 for North America. The box includes a total of 64 tracks across 4 LPs from the original soundtrack.

> The epic, orchestral soundtrack to Elden Ring: Nightreign is coming to vinyl for the first time as a 7LP set ... limited to 4999 copies.

> Yasumasa Kitagawa delivered a cinematic sci-fi score blending orchestral emotion with futuristic electronic sound design, with the full 46-track OST released in April 2026.

**Original scores of note.** GameSpot's 2026 best-soundtracks roundup highlights Ridiculon's (Matthias Bossi & Jon Evans) folksy Mewgenics score, Anamanaguchi's 71-song Scott Pilgrim soundtrack, the meditative Cairn score, and the genre-shifting Esoteric Ebb score [[the-best-video-game-soundtracks-of-2026-so-far-11501826]]. Sonic State published a January 2026 long-form interview with Mick Gordon covering his process, the DOOM/DOOM Eternal/Wolfenstein/Prey/Killer Instinct catalog, and reflections on creativity [[mick-gordon-in-conversation-sonic-state-jan-2026-53ed29e8]].

> The Mewgenics score combines a punchy, folksy sound with the inner workings of a cat's mind, crafted by Ridiculon's Matthias Bossi and Jon Evans.

> Mick Gordon participated in a 2026 conversation with Bonfire Conversations, discussing his life, fame, process, creativity and his childhood, as well as his career, creative process, and approach to music.

**Adaptive-audio middleware.** A 2026 UE5-focused comparison argues developers now have three serious options: Wwise as the AAA industry standard with the most sophisticated hierarchical interactive-music system (segments, playlists, switches), FMOD as the indie-friendly powerhouse, and Epic's built-in MetaSounds — workable for simple adaptive music but lacking dedicated music authoring tools, while offering zero licensing cost versus Wwise's per-platform commercial fees [[wwise-vs-fmod-vs-metasounds-choosing-audio-middleware-for-your-ue5-game-in-2026-9afdff3e]]. This feeds directly into the pre-declared `adaptive-music-tech-comparison` page.

> In 2026, Unreal Engine 5 developers have three serious options: Wwise (the AAA industry standard), FMOD (the indie-friendly powerhouse), and MetaSounds (Epic's built-in procedural audio system) ... MetaSounds is workable for simple adaptive music but lacks dedicated music authoring tools.

**Audio middleware — haptics convergence.** A May 2026 Meta developer post details Meta Haptics Studio's integration with both major audio-middleware packages, folding controller-haptic authoring into the sound designer's existing toolchain: FMOD supports the `.haptic` format today via the Haptics Instrument in FMOD 2.03.11, and Wwise gains native `.haptic` support in early 2026 [[seamless-haptics-for-sound-designers-meta-haptics-studio-meets-fmod-and-wwise-891ab31d]]. The post frames `.haptic` as a candidate de-facto interchange standard for controller haptics, analogous to `.wav` for audio, covering essentially every commercial VR title in production through the two middleware integrations [[seamless-haptics-for-sound-designers-meta-haptics-studio-meets-fmod-and-wwise-891ab31d]]. This extends the existing Wwise/FMOD/MetaSounds comparison from purely musical/SFX authoring into unified audio-plus-haptic feedback.

> FMOD support is available today through the Haptics Instrument in FMOD 2.03.11, while Wwise will natively support .haptic in early 2026.

> the .haptic format is on track to become the de facto interchange spec for controller haptics across platforms, the same way .wav became the universal audio interchange format.

**Composer interviews.** Screen Rant interviewed Donkey Kong Country composer David Wise at Gamescom LATAM 2026, where he performed on the main stage on May 1–2; the conversation covers his 40-year career from Rare (1985–2009) through ongoing NDA projects, with Wise stressing continuous tool-learning as central to his evolving process [[david-wise-on-creating-gaming-soundtracks-40-years-later-dc37403c]].

> With a career spanning over 40 years, the musician has created soundtracks for some of the most popular games ever. Wise began his career in games at Rare in 1985 and remained there until 2009. His best-known work is for the fantastic Donkey Kong Country, which was released by Rare in 1994 for the SNES.

**Live performance.** Distant Worlds: Music from FINAL FANTASY returns to the U.S. through 2026, including a two-night Carnegie Hall event on June 12–13, 2026, "Celebrating Nobuo Uematsu!", with a program chosen by Uematsu (in attendance) and vocalists RIKKI and Amanda Achen [[final-fantasy-distant-worlds-concerts-set-to-sail-through-the-u-s-in-2026-bd12486f]].

## Recent updates

**Original scores of note (cont.).** The second daily pass on 2026-05-21 surfaces RPGFan's mid-May review run. The standout new original score is *Aether & Iron*, co-composed by two-time Grammy winner Christopher Tin and Grammy-nominee Alex Williamson — a noir-leaning orchestral score (strings, horns, light percussion) recorded with a live orchestra, with RPGFan calling its "Nations Crusade Adagio" a choir-and-orchestra piece sitting "somewhere between the romantic era of classical composition and a good Hans Zimmer film score," and flagging the album as a plausible Grammy shortlist contender for next year [[aether-iron-soundtrack-music-review-5b0c61bb]]. On the JRPG side, Yasunori Nishiki's *Octopath Traveler 0 Original Soundtrack* (Square Enix, SQEX-11181~2, released 2025-12-10) spreads across 2 CDs, with the first disc built around the main theme and four elaborated variants ("Fury," "Sorrow," "Compassion," "Requiem") plus a full "Wishvale" town suite, while roughly 40 percent of the set is carried over from Champions of the Continent [[octopath-traveler-0-original-soundtrack-music-review-81d13f2c]].

> The Aether & Iron Soundtrack was co-composed by Christopher Tin and Alex Williamson. Tin is a two-time Grammy-winning composer, while Williamson is a Grammy-nominee.

> About 40 percent of the OST is music lifted from Champions of the Continent, all found on the second of the soundtrack's two CDs. The first disc features the main theme and four elaborated versions (Fury, Sorrow, Compassion, and Requiem).

**Arrangement releases.** Scarlet Moon Records' jazz duo GENTLE LOVE — saxophonist Norihiko Hibino (a Metal Gear Solid series composer) and pianist AYAKI — released their first all-Zelda album, *Prescription for Sleep: Ocarina of Time*, marking The Legend of Zelda's 40th anniversary; RPGFan characterizes it as roughly "25% Zelda, 75% GENTLE LOVE" with four-to-seven-minute tracks given over to soloing and calm sonic exploration [[prescription-for-sleep-ocarina-of-time-music-review-f69d5aa2]].

> Scarlet Moon Records' prolific jazz duo GENTLE LOVE released their first-ever all-Zelda album, Prescription for Sleep: Ocarina of Time, with saxophonist Norihiko Hibino (composer on the Metal Gear Solid series) and pianist AYAKI celebrating the 40th anniversary of The Legend of Zelda.

**Original scores of note (cont.).** The 2026-05-22 daily pass surfaces RPGFan's review of the *Blue Prince Original Soundtrack* by the Dutch duo Trigg & Gusset — lead composer Bart Knol with Erik van Geer co-composing on bass clarinet, their first game score [[blue-prince-original-soundtrack-music-review-13877127]]. The defining compositional choice is the total absence of drums or percussion, which RPGFan credits with enabling "excellent rubato" and a gentle, swaying ambient-jazz character; the closest the album comes to a pulse is the brisk 3/4 ostinato synth pattern of standout track "Under the Black Bridge" [[blue-prince-original-soundtrack-music-review-13877127]].

> One notable aspect of the Blue Prince OST is that it has no drums and no percussion of any kind. This allows for some excellent rubato and lets the listener get lost in the gentle, swaying tones without feeling that pulsing, bouncing energy.

> A standout track is Under the Black Bridge, a brisk 3/4 piece with an ostinato synth pattern that comes as close to percussion as the OST gets.

## Composer interviews (cont.) — 2026-05-23

The 2026-05-23 daily pass surfaces Game Informer's February 2026 interview with World of Warcraft lead composer Leo Kaliski on the score for the *Midnight* expansion. Kaliski frames Blizzard's music team as deliberately human-made amid the generative-AI wave, saying "We here at Blizzard feel very lucky and happy that we're not using generative AI. We're just writing what we think is cool" [[interview-world-of-warcraft-lead-composer-on-making-of-midnight-s-human-made-music-013afc37]]. He argues AI music still lags human work on perceptibility: "Music is not there yet. Usually, you hear it, and you instantly know something is not right about it, or the fidelity isn't there" [[interview-world-of-warcraft-lead-composer-on-making-of-midnight-s-human-made-music-013afc37]]. On craft, Kaliski describes WoW's deliberately broad musical identity as functional rather than foregrounded — the score sits in the background to support gameplay and story "without overstepping boundaries, unless it's a big cinematic moment" [[interview-world-of-warcraft-lead-composer-on-making-of-midnight-s-human-made-music-013afc37]].

> We here at Blizzard feel very lucky and happy that we're not using generative AI. We're just writing what we think is cool, and are happy to do that.

> Music is not there yet. Usually, you hear it, and you instantly know something is not right about it, or the fidelity isn't there.

**Original scores of note (cont.).** RPGFan's catalog adds two atmospheric OST reviews. The *Lies of P Original Soundtrack* spans 70 total tracks, the first 16 being collectible in-game records carrying lyrical ballads plus remixed "Golden" versions; RPGFan tags opener "Feel" as "rain-soaked smooth jazz" that sets the album apart from peer action-RPG scores, and notes the 2025 free addition of 51 Overture-DLC tracks to digital copies [[lies-of-p-original-soundtrack-music-review-94ceba96]]. Amos Roddy's *Citizen Sleeper Original Soundtrack* is a mellow 23-track slice-of-life score for a lived-in sci-fi setting, blending industrial, electro, and ambient textures RPGFan compares to Vangelis' *Blade Runner*, Kenji Kawai's *Ghost in the Shell*, and Kenji Yamamoto's *Metroid Prime* [[citizen-sleeper-original-soundtrack-music-review-25f6b6c7]].

> The soundtrack comprises 70 total tracks, with the first 16 being collectible records featuring lyrical ballads plus remixed Golden versions.

> The soundtrack is a mix of industrial, electro, and ambient sounds reminiscent of quieter moments from film and game soundtracks like Vangelis' Blade Runner (1982), Kenji Kawai's Ghost in the Shell (1995), and Kenji Yamamoto's Metroid Prime (2002).

**Awards.** The 2026 Grammy Award for Best Score Soundtrack for Video Games went to Austin Wintory for *Sword of the Sea*, over fellow nominees Pinar Toprak (Avatar: Frontiers of Pandora – Secrets of the Spires), Wilbert Roget (Helldivers 2), Gordy Haab (Indiana Jones and the Great Circle), and Cody Matthew Johnson & Wilbert Roget II (Star Wars Outlaws expansions) [[clair-obscur-snubbed-in-grammy-video-game-soundtrack-nominations-be8d1e4b]]. > Clair Obscur's soundtrack composed by Lorien Testard had topped the Billboard Classical charts for 10 entire weeks. Austin Wintory won the 2026 Grammy Award for Best Score Soundtrack for Video Games for his work on Sword of the Sea, dedicating his Grammy to Testard and Sandfall.

## Awards & releases — 2026-05-25

**Grammy snub — eligibility question resolved.** Game Informer's coverage of the 2026 Best Score Soundtrack for Video Games category clarifies the mechanics behind the Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 omission previously tracked on this page. The score was within the Academy's eligibility window (August 31, 2024 – August 30, 2025) and publisher Kepler actively submitted it in two categories — Best Score Soundtrack for Video Games and Best Song Written for Visual Media — so the omission was a nomination-process outcome, not an eligibility-window or non-submission technicality [[best-video-game-soundtrack-grammy-nominations-don-t-include-clair-obscur-expedition-33-e1f338df]]. The same piece documents how the window is governed by soundtrack release date rather than game launch: Helldivers 2 qualified despite launching before the window because its soundtrack dropped September 20, 2024, inside the window [[best-video-game-soundtrack-grammy-nominations-don-t-include-clair-obscur-expedition-33-e1f338df]].

> The 2026 Grammy Awards had an eligibility window of August 31, 2024 to August 30, 2025. Publisher Kepler submitted Clair Obscur for consideration in two Grammy categories: Best Score Soundtrack for Video Games, and Best Song Written for Visual Media.

> Helldivers 2 was able to qualify for the 2026 Grammys despite its game launching before the eligibility window, because its soundtrack was officially released on September 20, 2024, which fit within the Academy's window of August 31, 2024, to August 30, 2025.

**Vinyl releases (cont.).** Annapurna Interactive, Ivy Road, and iam8bit are pressing the Wanderstop soundtrack — C418's (Minecraft) lofi score — as a sunny-yellow 2xLP, alongside a companion seed-pink "Wanderstop FM" 2xLP collecting the game's in-game animal-themed radio tracks; both ship August 28, 2026 with new artwork from Nat F [[wanderstop-soundtrack-to-get-vinyl-release-d0810a43]]. The pressing extends the 2026 vinyl-revival pattern already documented for Chrono Trigger, Elden Ring: Nightreign, Pragmata, MOUSE: P.I., and Ghost of Yotei to a marquee cozy-game / indie-electronic score.

> The game's lofi soundtrack from Minecraft composer C418 will be available as a 2LP set pressed on sunny yellow vinyl, while the animal-themed songs that make up Wanderstop's in-game radio are also being released on seed pink vinyl. The vinyl sets will ship August 28, 2026.

**Retro-revival score.** Incube8 Games shipped Infinity for Game Boy Color in 2026 — an RPG originally built by Affinix Software from 1999 to 2001 and long one of retro gaming's famous "what-ifs" — and RPGFan reviewed its arranged album, which features a guest arrangement by Yuzo Koshiro (Ys, ActRaiser, Etrian Odyssey). RPGFan singles out Koshiro's rendition of "Another Challenger Enters" as a guitar-shredding rock/metal high point of the arrange album, alongside lead composer Eric E. Hache's chiptune originals [[infinity-arranged-soundtrack-music-review-2a862cf8]].

> Legendary composer Yuzo Koshiro (Ys, ActRaiser, Etrian Odyssey) gets a guest spot! The Infinity arrange album features several new arranged tracks, with a few recorded in 2001 and circulated through demos released by composer Eric Hache at the time.

## Releases & composers — 2026-05-26

**Composer reveal — Silent Hill returns to synth-horror.** Konami's *Silent Hill: Townfall* named Canadian musician and filmmaker Pilotpriest (Anthony Scott Burns) as composer, with the first track "Home" debuting April 7, 2026; Burns is best known for directing and scoring the psychological-horror film *Come True* [[first-song-from-silent-hill-townfall-soundtrack-has-been-released-d5fb972f]]. The craft angle of note is succession: Burns explicitly cites Akira Yamaoka's foundational Silent Hill soundscape — warbly ethereal drones, hypnotic melodies, stark industrial percussion, and dirt-laden frequencies — as his reference point, framing the commission as a chance to stay "entirely true to the spirit of the series" [[first-song-from-silent-hill-townfall-soundtrack-has-been-released-d5fb972f]].

> Pilotpriest (Anthony Scott Burns) is composing the soundtrack to Silent Hill: Townfall, with the first track 'Home' debuting April 7, 2026 ... Burns cited Akira Yamaoka's haunting Silent Hill score — warbly ethereal drones, hypnotic melodies, stark industrial percussion, and dirt-laden frequencies — as foundational ... creating something emotionally honest, rooted in fear, and entirely true to the spirit of the series.

**Distribution model — Nintendo Music gates a pre-release score.** Nintendo Music added a 10-track "Special Release" of the upcoming Switch 2 *Star Fox* (out June 25, 2026) ahead of the game's launch, accessible only with an active Switch Online membership [[nintendo-music-special-release-adds-tracks-from-star-fox-s-switch-2-game-930581c4]]. This is a notable distribution-model data point: a marquee score released as a subscription-gated streaming drop rather than as a standalone OST or vinyl, contrasting with the standalone-vinyl pattern dominating the 2026 cycle tracked here.

> 10 songs from the upcoming Star Fox were released as a 'Special Release' on the Nintendo Music app; to listen, you'll need an active Switch Online membership. Star Fox is scheduled for a Switch 2 release on 25th June 2026.

**Archival availability — Namco × Capcom hits streaming after 20 years.** Yuzo Koshiro confirmed that the 2005 PS2 *Namco × Capcom* soundtrack — co-composed with Soichiro Morizumi, Takuji Kawano, Kazue Saito, and Kazunori Haruyama for Monolith Soft — reached streaming services for the first time starting April 11, 2026, ending two decades of official unavailability [[we-kept-you-waiting-yuzo-koshiro-confirms-namco-capcom-s-soundtrack-is-making-a-partial-comeback-d3156b39]]. This tracks a distinct sub-pattern — back-catalog streaming reissues — alongside the new-release vinyl revival.

> Yuzo Koshiro confirmed that after about 20 years since the game's 2005 release, the Namco × Capcom soundtrack is finally being made available for streaming, beginning on various services starting April 11, 2026 ... the first time the soundtrack has been officially available on streaming platforms after being unavailable for two decades.

**Vinyl revival (cont.) — Grim Fandango 25th-anniversary repress.** iam8bit, with Double Fine and Disney, is repressing Peter McConnell's *Grim Fandango Original Game Soundtrack* as a 51-track 2xLP for the game's 25th anniversary; RPGFan characterizes McConnell's "big band, bebop, and bones" score as the best jazz-based video game soundtrack it has heard, praising the use of genuine instruments over synthesized instrumentation [[grim-fandango-original-game-soundtrack-music-review-a5a43d07]].

> The soundtrack features Peter McConnell's 'big band, bebop, and bones' style ... easily the best jazz-based video game soundtrack they've ever heard ... iam8bit, in partnership with Double Fine and Disney, is repressing the Grim Fandango 2xLP Vinyl Soundtrack — 51 tracks pressed on two vintage black vinyl discs — for the game's 25th anniversary.

## Releases & milestones — 2026-05-28

**Composer interview (cont.) — Jake Kaufman details the Mina the Hollower chiptune craft.** A So Many Games feature interview adds substantial craft detail to the Mina the Hollower score already tracked on this page. Kaufman composed the soundtrack in Furnace Tracker, configured for Game Boy + MSX-SCC chips, producing more than 100 tracks for the game, with two guest tracks from Yuzo Koshiro [[burrowing-under-the-mina-the-hollower-soundtrack-an-interview-with-jake-kaufman-ba06821d]]. The most personal compositional choice is what Kaufman calls 'Cajunvania': two areas — Septemburg and Nox's Bayou — were shaped by his parents' musical tastes, his father's fingerstyle folk guitar and his mother's love of Zydeco, with 'Gator Gallop' from Nox's Bayou as his standout track [[burrowing-under-the-mina-the-hollower-soundtrack-an-interview-with-jake-kaufman-ba06821d]]. This refines the earlier 'MSX-style score' framing into a precise toolchain (Furnace Tracker / Game Boy + MSX-SCC) and confirms Koshiro's involvement.

> The soundtrack was created in Furnace Tracker (multi-system, but Game Boy + MSX-SCC in this case), with more than 100 tracks for the game and two guest tracks from Yuzo Koshiro.

> Two areas in the game, Septemburg and Nox's Bayou, were inspired by his parents' musical tastes — his father's fingerstyle folk guitar and his mother's love of Zydeco — resulting in 'Cajunvania' tracks; 'Gator Gallop' from Nox's Bayou is his standout track.

**Music history milestone — Doom OST enters the US National Recording Registry.** The Library of Congress inducted Bobby Prince's 1993 Doom soundtrack into the National Recording Registry, selected from more than 3,000 public nominations as one of 25 recordings designated 'audio treasures worthy of preservation' [[the-original-doom-soundtrack-joins-our-national-playlist-in-the-us-library-of-congress-b4da2399]]. Doom is only the third video-game-music entry ever chosen, following the Super Mario Bros. theme and Minecraft: Volume Alpha — a notable institutional-recognition data point for game music as a preserved art form. The craft angle of note: Prince wrote all the music before the game had finished levels and ensured his sound effects could cut through the music by assigning them to different MIDI frequencies — an early hand-rolled solution to the SFX-vs-music separation problem that adaptive-audio middleware now handles via mixer buses [[the-original-doom-soundtrack-joins-our-national-playlist-in-the-us-library-of-congress-b4da2399]].

> The Library of Congress inducted Bobby Prince's 1993 Doom soundtrack into the National Recording Registry, selected alongside 24 other recordings from more than 3,000 public nominations. Doom is only the third video game music entry chosen, following the Super Mario Bros. theme and Minecraft: Volume Alpha.

> All of the music was written before the game had completed levels, and despite the limitations of 1993-era sound card drivers, Prince ensured that his sound effects could cut through the music by assigning them to different MIDI frequencies.

**Retro-revival score — full remake of the 1994 Elder Scrolls: Arena soundtrack.** Independent composer Ryan Zachariah Martin remade the entire soundtrack of 1994's The Elder Scrolls: Arena, originally composed by Eric Heberling, using software unavailable in 1994 to present the tracks as they were originally conceived [[this-composer-just-remade-the-entire-soundtrack-of-the-first-elder-scrolls-game-297eb7c2]]. Martin frames the project as fidelity-to-intent rather than reinvention, saying he is 'most proud of the character creation track' and that for the intro theme he aimed to capture 'the feel of what the intro was originally aiming for, with an emphasis on the beauty and simplicity of the melodies, rather than a bombastic orchestral piece' [[this-composer-just-remade-the-entire-soundtrack-of-the-first-elder-scrolls-game-297eb7c2]]. This is a distinct sub-pattern from the 2026 vinyl revival thread: a fan/independent full-score reconstruction of an early-era soundtrack.

> A composer just remade the entire soundtrack of the first Elder Scrolls game, using software unavailable in 1994 to present the tracks as they were originally conceived, removing the technical barriers that previously impacted the listening experience.

> I am most proud of the character creation track ... I think I captured the feel of what the intro was originally aiming for, with an emphasis on the beauty and simplicity of the melodies, rather than a bombastic orchestral piece.

## Releases & craft — 2026-05-29

**Soundtrack drop — Mina the Hollower OST releases standalone on launch day.** Resolving open questions from 2026-05-27 and 2026-05-28, Yacht Club Games released Jake Kaufman's full Mina the Hollower score as a standalone 96-track OST the same day the game launched, available as a Name Your Price (free-if-you-want) Bandcamp album and as Steam DLC [[mina-the-hollower-launches-on-consoles-and-pc-soundtrack-now-available-f50145d8]]. The release confirms the precise sound architecture: the Game Boy sound chip combined with the Konami Sound Cartridge (052539 / SCC+), an MSX expansion, authored in Furnace Tracker, with Yuzo Koshiro's two guest contributions pinned to tracks 57 and 63 [[mina-the-hollower-launches-on-consoles-and-pc-soundtrack-now-available-f50145d8]].

> Yacht Club Games has released Mina the Hollower on consoles and PC alongside a 96-track original soundtrack now available on Bandcamp. Fans can spend whatever amount they want to obtain this soundtrack in various formats, such as MP3 and FLAC; it is also available as DLC on Steam. Mina's soundtrack uses the Game Boy sound chip combined with the Konami Sound Cartridge (052539 / SCC+), an expansion for the MSX computer, with tracks 57 and 63 written by Yuzo Koshiro.

**Original score of note — Scott Pilgrim EX is Anamanaguchi's most expansive game work.** RPGFan's dedicated review of the Scott Pilgrim EX Original Soundtrack upgrades the passing mention in GameSpot's 2026 roundup: 71 tracks across roughly 90 minutes, with each screen and shop getting its own sub-minute looping cue, marking Anamanaguchi's biggest and most sonically expansive game-soundtrack project yet [[scott-pilgrim-ex-original-soundtrack-music-review-7d0adae5]]. RPGFan singles out 'One More Summer' (the first main quest area's bright track, framed as the successor to 'Another Winter' from Scott Pilgrim vs. the World: The Game) and the triumphant closer 'United by Fate' as standouts [[scott-pilgrim-ex-original-soundtrack-music-review-7d0adae5]]. A Polyvinyl 2xLP pressing (Sky Blue & Hot Pink Blend, deluxe gatefold) is slated to ship August 7, 2026, extending the 2026 vinyl-revival pattern tracked on this page to a chiptune/pop-rock indie score.

> The soundtrack features 71 tracks across an hour and a half, with each screen and shop having its own short looping track; most tracks loop in under a minute. 'One More Summer,' a bright track for the first main quest area, fills the void left since 'Another Winter' from Scott Pilgrim vs. the World: The Game, and 'United by Fate' is a triumphant culmination harkening to Anamanaguchi's joyful nostalgia.

**Adaptive scoring beyond video games — tabletop RPG cue design.** RPGFan's feature on composer Josh Barron's The Lost Mines of Phandelver score documents an adaptive cue architecture applied to a tabletop RPG session: distinct town, nature-environment, dungeon, standard-battle, and boss-battle beds, plus musical stings/jingles for quest completion and leveling up — the same vertical-remixing / stinger vocabulary used in video-game adaptive-audio systems, re-applied to a human-run TTRPG [[music-for-tabletop-rpgs-scoring-your-adventure-with-phandelver-41448781]].

> The Lost Mines of Phandelver soundtrack by composer Josh Barron features town music, pretty nature environmental themes, scary dungeon themes, standard battle music, and special boss battle music, with musical stings/jingles for quest completion and leveling up.

**Industry trend — the composer as systems designer amid generative audio.** An AudioNewsRoom analysis argues game composers are shifting "from delivering files to designing systems," with most studios landing on a hybrid model — AI for procedural variation, humans for core creative vision [[game-music-is-changing-the-composer-s-new-role-65910821]]. The piece tempers cost-savings hype: generative audio cuts production cost by over 50% only in narrow cases (e.g. ambient procedural content in a 100-hour open-world game), and hides new costs in specialized talent, software licensing, and engine performance overhead; fully AI-generated content can't be copyrighted in the US, and contract clauses shielding composer work from AI training are now standard [[game-music-is-changing-the-composer-s-new-role-65910821]].

> The hybrid model — AI for procedural variation, humans for core creative vision — is where most studios are landing. While generative audio cuts production costs by over 50% in narrow circumstances ... this hides new costs like specialized talent, software licensing, and engine performance overhead. Copyright remains genuinely unsettled: in the US, fully AI-generated content can't be copyrighted, and contract clauses protecting composer work from AI training are now standard practice.

## Jazz arrangements — 2026-05-30

**Square Enix Jazz –Chrono Trigger– closes a five-album series.** RPGFan reviewed the final entry in Square Enix's 2017–2022 dedicated jazz series (six albums if the live entry is counted), which is uniquely dedicated to a single SNES RPG [[square-enix-jazz-chrono-trigger-music-review-e41b23fa]]. The album's 11 tracks were arranged by trombonist Eijiro Nakagawa and bassist Ryu Kawamura, aiming to be "equal parts nostalgic and fresh" [[square-enix-jazz-chrono-trigger-music-review-e41b23fa]]. The instrumentation stays within a traditional jazz combo — brass, piano, percussion, and standing bass — keeping the arrangements loyal to jazz fundamentals rather than genre-blending [[square-enix-jazz-chrono-trigger-music-review-e41b23fa]].

The review's most notable critical framing is its definition of jazz quality: "Jazz arrangements are powerful because jazz is largely defined by its heart — not a sterile adherence to sheet music with an emphasis on precision, but emotion" [[square-enix-jazz-chrono-trigger-music-review-e41b23fa]]. This parallels the Prescription for Sleep: Ocarina of Time entry above (GENTLE LOVE's approach to Zelda material) in treating emotional latitude as the defining criterion for jazz-arrangement success. The only complaint lodged is the omission of 'Secret of the Forest,' though the reviewer acknowledges that "with what is arguably the best gaming soundtrack ever created, almost any track from the OST deserves a spot" [[square-enix-jazz-chrono-trigger-music-review-e41b23fa]].

> From 2017 to 2022, Square Enix produced five albums in a dedicated Square Enix Jazz series (six if you include a live album). The final album in this set is a full jazz album solely for the classic SNES RPG Chrono Trigger.

> Jazz arrangements are powerful because jazz is largely defined by its heart — not a sterile adherence to sheet music with an emphasis on precision, but emotion.

> The only complaint is the omission of 'Secret of the Forest,' but with what is arguably the best gaming soundtrack ever created, almost any track from the OST deserves a spot.
