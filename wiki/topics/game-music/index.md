## Summary

2026 in game music is defined by several intertwined currents: a sweeping **vinyl-revival cycle** — now surfacing its first composer-consent-and-compensation controversy — an intensifying **AI-versus-human-composer** debate, a crowded **live-concert circuit** anchored in London, maturing **adaptive-audio middleware** practice, a wave of **franchise-composer handoffs**, and an **awards** season marked by a high-profile Grammy snub. The lossless chronology lives in `## Recent updates` below; this section is the current-state synthesis.

**Vinyl revival is the dominant release pattern.** Catalog and new-release scores alike are being pressed to vinyl at scale — Square Enix's 30th-anniversary 4xLP Chrono Trigger box [[chrono-trigger-original-soundtrack-vinyl-box-to-release-in-april-2026-f0889eea]], Bandai Namco's 7LP Elden Ring: Nightreign [[elden-ring-nightreign-soundtrack-is-coming-to-vinyl-30e2c324]], and a steady 2026 cadence spanning MOUSE: P.I. [[mouse-p-i-for-hire-soundtrack-hits-streaming-ahead-of-vinyl-release-8ebe2f29]], Ghost of Yotei [[ghost-of-yotei-soundtrack-is-getting-a-vinyl-release-fb6a8f99]], Wanderstop [[wanderstop-soundtrack-to-get-vinyl-release-d0810a43]], Scott Pilgrim EX [[scott-pilgrim-ex-original-soundtrack-music-review-7d0adae5]], Cyberpunk 2077 [[cyberpunk-2077-future-sound-of-night-city-officially-revealed-coming-june-2026-9d8dd817]], Mewgenics [[mewgenics-soundtrack-is-coming-to-vinyl-2e9ffcd5]], LittleBigPlanet/Sackboy [[littlebigplanet-and-sackboy-a-big-adventure-vinyl-soundtracks-up-for-preorder-via-iam8bit-890918f1]], Atelier Ryza [[koei-tecmo-europe-and-black-screen-records-announce-the-atelier-ryza-official-soundtrack-trilogy-vinyl-box-set-316fa31f]], Stellar Blade [[stellar-blade-original-soundtrack-vinyl-pre-orders-are-now-live-3bf50292]], Nintendo/Laced's 8-LP Breath of the Wild box [[nintendo-is-releasing-a-massive-breath-of-the-wild-vinyl-soundtrack-collection-on-eight-lps-2b1e4aa6]], a notable Japanese-catalog first in Materia Collective's deluxe 2xLP 'Golden Epitaph' pressing of *Umineko no Naku Koro Ni* (zts compositions, 07th Expansion-approved, $39.99, shipping Q3 2026), the first official vinyl release of Umineko's music outside Japan [[umineko-no-naku-koro-ni-original-soundtrack-selections-vol-1-vinyl-pre-orders-open-7e5c06bc]], and — the latest flagship-catalog entry — Laced Records' 8LP 'Spartan Green' box collecting 83 tracks from the original Halo trilogy ahead of the *Halo: Campaign Evolved* remake (~$190, shipping ~February 2027) [[halo-soundtrack-getting-vinyl-release-ahead-of-campaign-evolved-remake-9240e484]]. A recurring tension across cozy/ambient scores is that adaptive mood-setting music doesn't always translate to sit-and-listen vinyl [[nintendo-is-releasing-a-massive-breath-of-the-wild-vinyl-soundtrack-collection-on-eight-lps-2b1e4aa6]].

> To commemorate the 30th anniversary of Chrono Trigger, Square Enix will release a vinyl box set of the game's original soundtrack on March 25, 2026 in Japan and April 2026 for North America. The box includes a total of 64 tracks across 4 LPs from the original soundtrack.

> Breath of the Wild is an interesting choice for an initial vinyl release, given that the game's soundtrack is known for mood-setting vibes but not really known for bops.

**The newest fault line in the vinyl cycle is composer consent and compensation.** The Halo reissue is the first time the composer of a flagship catalog score has openly contested a 2026 vinyl release: original Halo composer Marty O'Donnell — who scored the trilogy with Michael Salvatori — says he was not consulted, that Microsoft does not hold the original multitrack recordings (making the "remastered" label misleading because a true remaster is impossible), and most pointedly that "none of the composers or performers will see a dime" from the release [[halo-composer-hits-out-at-vinyl-release-none-of-the-performers-will-see-a-dime-6a0e1792]]. This sharpens a question the rest of the year's publisher-driven catalog pressings have left implicit — who is paid and who approves when a publisher presses a decades-old score it now controls. O'Donnell and Salvatori previously litigated (2020) and then "amicably resolved" (April 2022) a separate royalties suit against Microsoft [[halo-composer-hits-out-at-vinyl-release-none-of-the-performers-will-see-a-dime-6a0e1792]].

> O'Donnell noted that Microsoft doesn't have the original multitrack recordings, making remastering impossible despite Microsoft's claims. Most notably, 'None of the composers or performers will see a dime' from the vinyl release, according to O'Donnell's statement on social media.

**The AI question is the defining industry tension.** Three distinct threat framings co-exist: a quality/perceptibility gap (WoW lead composer Leo Kaliski insists generative music is still "not there yet" [[interview-world-of-warcraft-lead-composer-on-making-of-midnight-s-human-made-music-013afc37]]), copyright risk and streaming-platform saturation (NME's survey with Austin Wintory and Lena Raine [[ai-vinyl-and-indie-supremacy-whats-shaping-video-game-music-in-2026-fadb3642]]), and an economic/structural shift toward the "composer as systems designer" under a hybrid AI-for-procedural-variation / human-for-core-vision model [[game-music-is-changing-the-composer-s-new-role-65910821]]. AI-free contract clauses are now standard practice [[ai-vinyl-and-indie-supremacy-whats-shaping-video-game-music-in-2026-fadb3642]]. The competing framings are tracked as an open dispute below.

> Music is not there yet. Usually, you hear it, and you instantly know something is not right about it, or the fidelity isn't there.

> The hybrid model — AI for procedural variation, humans for core creative vision — is where most studios are landing. While generative audio cuts production costs by over 50% in narrow circumstances ... this hides new costs like specialized talent, software licensing, and engine performance overhead.

**Live performance is booming around a London hub.** Game Music Festival 2026 at the Royal Festival Hall opens with The Infernal Symphony (Diablo 30th) [[the-infernal-symphony-diablo-30th-anniversary-concert-game-music-festival-2026-8d094ebf]] and closes with Ballads of the Underworld (Hades/Hades II) [[ballads-of-the-underworld-concert-featuring-music-from-hades-hades-ii-3da356ea]]; the same festival hosts Hitoshi Sakimoto's 40th-anniversary "Colours of Harmony" concert [[london-mozart-players-the-colours-of-harmony-hitoshi-sakimoto-40th-anniversary-celebration-d3196284]] [[final-fantasy-tactics-composer-hitoshi-sakimoto-reveals-fft-is-rock-6f1175e4]]. Around it: BAFTA Games in Concert [[bafta-games-in-concert-set-to-premiere-at-londons-royal-festival-hall-2f16568a]], Distant Worlds at Carnegie Hall [[final-fantasy-distant-worlds-concerts-set-to-sail-through-the-u-s-in-2026-bd12486f]], Uematsu's first concert-hall work Merregnon: Heart of Ice [[decca-classics-announces-first-original-concert-work-by-nobuo-uematsu-merregnon-heart-of-ice-ecc81ed5]], and the 30th-anniversary Persona Live tour [[review-persona-live-2026-awakenings-brought-the-vibes-in-first-batch-of-u-s-concerts-63f4bb81]]. Austin Wintory is emerging as the de facto game-music conductor laureate.

The legendary themes will be woven into a monumental new opus arranged by Ignacy Wojciechowski, with special guests Lead Composer Ted Reedy and Music Director Derek Duke.

**Adaptive-audio middleware: Wwise / FMOD / MetaSounds.** The standing best-practice guidance for UE5 in 2026 frames Wwise as the AAA standard, FMOD as the indie-friendly powerhouse, and MetaSounds as workable-but-limited for music [[wwise-vs-fmod-vs-metasounds-choosing-audio-middleware-for-your-ue5-game-in-2026-9afdff3e]]; this is now extending into unified audio-plus-haptics via Meta Haptics Studio and the emerging `.haptic` interchange format [[seamless-haptics-for-sound-designers-meta-haptics-studio-meets-fmod-and-wwise-891ab31d]].

> In 2026, Unreal Engine 5 developers have three serious options: Wwise (the AAA industry standard), FMOD (the indie-friendly powerhouse), and MetaSounds (Epic's built-in procedural audio system) ... MetaSounds is workable for simple adaptive music but lacks dedicated music authoring tools.

**Notable scores & craft.** Standouts across segments: chiptune — Jake Kaufman's 100+-track Mina the Hollower (Furnace Tracker, Game Boy + MSX-SCC, Yuzo Koshiro guests) [[burrowing-under-the-mina-the-hollower-soundtrack-an-interview-with-jake-kaufman-ba06821d]] [[mina-the-hollower-launches-on-consoles-and-pc-soundtrack-now-available-f50145d8]]; orchestral/noir — Aether & Iron (Christopher Tin) [[aether-iron-soundtrack-music-review-5b0c61bb]]; jazz arrangements — Square Enix Jazz –Chrono Trigger– [[square-enix-jazz-chrono-trigger-music-review-e41b23fa]] and the Grim Fandango 25th-anniversary repress [[grim-fandango-original-game-soundtrack-music-review-a5a43d07]]; and music-as-mechanic — Glee-Cheese's Mr. Records, where the music *is* the level geometry [[after-mixtape-cheerful-music-game-mr-records-is-a-day-of-the-devs-standout-7995adb8]] [[mr-records-your-questions-answered-36564162]].

> MR. RECORDS blends record shop management, musical journeys, and heartfelt storytelling into an experience entirely powered by the transformative magic of music. The soundtrack covers a wide range of original music, including funk, progressive rock, hip hop, electro, world music, and 80s pop, composed by the team at Glee-Cheese themselves.

**Composer continuity vs. generational handoff.** Two patterns coexist when long-running franchises return. Some bring the original composer back, often paired with a marquee co-composer — Christophe Héral returns for the Rayman Legends remake alongside Grant Kirkhope (Banjo-Kazooie, DK64), with 55 minutes of new music [[the-rayman-legends-remake-has-brought-on-banjo-kazooie-and-dk64-composer-grant-kirkhope-who-can-apparently-spin-up-gold-in-a-day-921a10e6]]. Others stage a deliberate generational handoff. Stewart Copeland — composer of the first four mainline Spyro games and the 2018 Reignited Trilogy — confirmed he is *not* returning for *Spyro: A Realm Beyond*, framing it as an intentional handoff: "It's a new generation. They want a new generation of sound… they've got to modernise it" [[the-police-stewart-copeland-won-t-be-back-for-new-spyro-soundtrack-776aefc6]]. This mirrors the David Wise → Naoto Kubo transition on *Donkey Kong Bananza*, where a Tokyo-based team takes a live-orchestra-first approach to DK's identity (Wise's lineage acknowledged but not extended) [[dk-rap-composer-on-tracks-inclusion-in-donkey-kong-bananza-surprised-people-like-it-now-fda1fd79]] [[david-wise-on-creating-gaming-soundtracks-40-years-later-dc37403c]].

> When explaining his decision, Copeland stated: 'It's a new generation. They want a new generation of sound and everything… they've got to modernise it.'

> The music of Donkey Kong Bananza was composed by Naoto Kubo (as sound director and music lead), Daisuke Matsuoka, Reika Nakai, Yuri Goto and Tsukasa Usui. In a series first, Bananza uses mainly live-performed orchestra with some rock elements.

**Recent OST reviews.** RPGFan's mid-2026 review slate adds two notable entries. *Stellar Blade Arranged Tracks* (14 tracks, ~60 min) splits cleanly into a jazzy first half and an orchestral second half opening with 'The Song of the Sirens'; the Youngjee vocal version of 'Don't Forget Me' is the standout, while the orchestral 'Star Descent' is the weakest track — "too many layers, rhythms, and melodies that change too much" [[stellar-blade-arranged-tracks-music-review-628f3c65]] (the source OST's vinyl pre-order is tracked in the vinyl thread above [[stellar-blade-original-soundtrack-vinyl-pre-orders-are-now-live-3bf50292]]). *Citizen Sleeper 2: Starward Vector* (Amos Roddy, 34 tracks, ~1h41m) is judged more compositionally ambitious than the original — wider dynamic range, suited to the sequel's shift from conglomerate-choked cyberpunk to free-range space exploration — but "doesn't exactly arrive at new and exciting places the same way the first did," a masterfully produced score that is nonetheless less revelatory than CS1's fresher identity [[citizen-sleeper-2-starward-vector-soundtrack-music-review-3bac9e04]] [[citizen-sleeper-original-soundtrack-music-review-25f6b6c7]].

> The album acts as somewhat of a divider, splitting the jazzy first half from the more orchestral second half. The orchestral arrangements begin with 'The Song of the Sirens,' which employs one of the most memorable motifs from the main Stellar Blade OST.

> Rather than strictly neon-tinged, conglomerate-choked cyberpunk, CS2 opts to be more spacey, ambient, and doe-eyed in places, coinciding with the game's more free-range exploration of the stars. It is a masterfully produced soundtrack that complements and enhances what you experience in-game.

**Awards & recognition.** Austin Wintory won the 2026 Grammy for Best Score Soundtrack for Video Games (Sword of the Sea); the season's defining story was the omission of Lorien Testard's Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 score despite 10 weeks atop Billboard Classical and confirmed eligibility and two-category submission [[clair-obscur-snubbed-in-grammy-video-game-soundtrack-nominations-be8d1e4b]] [[best-video-game-soundtrack-grammy-nominations-don-t-include-clair-obscur-expedition-33-e1f338df]] [[clair-obscur-expedition-33-interview-with-composer-lorien-testard-c452744e]]. The new Ivors Composers Awards add a dedicated Video Games category [[the-new-ivors-composers-awards-will-honor-video-game-composers-b633147b]], and Bobby Prince's 1993 Doom score entered the US National Recording Registry [[the-original-doom-soundtrack-joins-our-national-playlist-in-the-us-library-of-congress-b4da2399]].

> Clair Obscur's soundtrack composed by Lorien Testard had topped the Billboard Classical charts for 10 entire weeks. Austin Wintory won the 2026 Grammy Award for Best Score Soundtrack for Video Games for his work on Sword of the Sea, dedicating his Grammy to Testard and Sandfall.

**In memoriam — Bobby Prince (1944/45–2026).** Robert Caskin "Bobby" Prince III, composer of the classic id Software and 3D Realms FPS catalog — Doom, Doom II, Wolfenstein 3D, Duke Nukem 3D, and Quake — died June 16, 2026 at the age of 81; his work is widely credited with helping legitimize video game music as an art form, and he received a Game Industry Lifetime Achievement Award in 2006 [[bobby-prince-composer-of-classic-id-software-and-3drealms-fps-video-game-music-dead-at-81-c5fbea75]]. His passing coincides with one of the field's highest formal recognitions: his 1993 Doom soundtrack was selected for preservation in the US Library of Congress's National Recording Registry in 2026 — only the third video-game-music entry ever chosen [[the-original-doom-soundtrack-joins-our-national-playlist-in-the-us-library-of-congress-b4da2399]]. Industry figures paid tribute: Doom co-designer John Romero wrote that "He left an incredible mark on games and on my life," and 3D Realms co-founder George Broussard called Prince "the epitome of a Southern gentleman," recalling that Prince would fly in for week-long stints on large projects like Duke Nukem 3D to "dial in what sort of music was appropriate" [[bobby-prince-the-legendary-composer-behind-doom-and-wolfenstein-3d-has-died-88b607d4]].

> Robert Caskin "Bobby" Prince III passed away peacefully on June 16, 2026, at the age of 81. He worked on classic FPS video games Doom, Doom II, Wolfenstein 3D, Quake, and Duke Nukem 3D back in the 1990s and his work helped legitimize video game music as an artform.

> John Romero, the Doom co-designer, paid tribute to Bobby Prince, writing 'Everyone at Romero Games is deeply saddened to learn of the passing of Bobby Prince. He left an incredible mark on games and on my life.'

**Industry trend — the composer as systems designer amid generative audio.** An AudioNewsRoom analysis argues game composers are shifting "from delivering files to designing systems," with most studios landing on a hybrid model — AI for procedural variation, humans for core creative vision [[game-music-is-changing-the-composer-s-new-role-65910821]]. The piece tempers cost-savings hype: generative audio cuts production cost by over 50% only in narrow cases (e.g. ambient procedural content in a 100-hour open-world game), and hides new costs in specialized talent, software licensing, and engine performance overhead; fully AI-generated content can't be copyrighted in the US, and contract clauses shielding composer work from AI training are now standard [[game-music-is-changing-the-composer-s-new-role-65910821]].

> The hybrid model — AI for procedural variation, humans for core creative vision — is where most studios are landing. While generative audio cuts production costs by over 50% in narrow circumstances ... this hides new costs like specialized talent, software licensing, and engine performance overhead. Copyright remains genuinely unsettled: in the US, fully AI-generated content can't be copyrighted, and contract clauses protecting composer work from AI training are now standard practice.

### Updates 2026-05-28 — Releases & milestones

**Composer interview (cont.) — Jake Kaufman details the Mina the Hollower chiptune craft.** A So Many Games feature interview adds substantial craft detail to the Mina the Hollower score already tracked on this page. > The soundtrack was created in Furnace Tracker (multi-system, but Game Boy + MSX-SCC in this case), with more than 100 tracks for the game and two guest tracks from Yuzo Koshiro.

**Music history milestone — Doom OST enters the US National Recording Registry.** The Library of Congress inducted Bobby Prince's 1993 Doom soundtrack into the National Recording Registry, selected from more than 3,000 public nominations as one of 25 recordings designated 'audio treasures worthy of preservation' [[the-original-doom-soundtrack-joins-our-national-playlist-in-the-us-library-of-congress-b4da2399]]. Doom is only the third video-game-music entry ever chosen, following the Super Mario Bros. theme and Minecraft: Volume Alpha — a notable institutional-recognition data point for game music as a preserved art form. The craft angle of note: Prince wrote all the music before the game had finished levels and ensured his sound effects could cut through the music by assigning them to different MIDI frequencies — an early hand-rolled solution to the SFX-vs-music separation problem that adaptive-audio middleware now handles via mixer buses [[the-original-doom-soundtrack-joins-our-national-playlist-in-the-us-library-of-congress-b4da2399]].

> The Library of Congress inducted Bobby Prince's 1993 Doom soundtrack into the National Recording Registry, selected alongside 24 other recordings from more than 3,000 public nominations. Doom is only the third video game music entry chosen, following the Super Mario Bros. theme and Minecraft: Volume Alpha.

> All of the music was written before the game had completed levels, and despite the limitations of 1993-era sound card drivers, Prince ensured that his sound effects could cut through the music by assigning them to different MIDI frequencies.

**Retro-revival score — full remake of the 1994 Elder Scrolls: Arena soundtrack.** Independent composer Ryan Zachariah Martin remade the entire soundtrack of 1994's The Elder Scrolls: Arena, originally composed by Eric Heberling, using software unavailable in 1994 to present the tracks as they were originally conceived [[this-composer-just-remade-the-entire-soundtrack-of-the-first-elder-scrolls-game-297eb7c2]]. Martin frames the project as fidelity-to-intent rather than reinvention, saying he is 'most proud of the character creation track' and that for the intro theme he aimed to capture 'the feel of what the intro was originally aiming for, with an emphasis on the beauty and simplicity of the melodies, rather than a bombastic orchestral piece' [[this-composer-just-remade-the-entire-soundtrack-of-the-first-elder-scrolls-game-297eb7c2]]. This is a distinct sub-pattern from the 2026 vinyl revival thread: a fan/independent full-score reconstruction of an early-era soundtrack.

> A composer just remade the entire soundtrack of the first Elder Scrolls game, using software unavailable in 1994 to present the tracks as they were originally conceived, removing the technical barriers that previously impacted the listening experience.

> I am most proud of the character creation track ... I think I captured the feel of what the intro was originally aiming for, with an emphasis on the beauty and simplicity of the melodies, rather than a bombastic orchestral piece.
