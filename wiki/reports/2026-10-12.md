## Today's brief

The headline finding today is **Bose Corporation's acquisition of Firelight Technologies** (FMOD's maker), announced October 1, 2026 — the first ownership change for either of the two dominant game-audio middleware platforms (FMOD / Wwise). Bose pledges the platform "will remain open and broadly supported," with gaming as the initial focus and longer-term VR/AR and automotive exploration; the deal is the second leg of a B2B interactive-audio strategy after the June 2026 StreamUnlimited acquisition. Practical questions (pricing, licensing tiers, engine-integration roadmap) remain unanswered. For the game-dev stack the main action was **NVIDIA refreshing the In-Game Inference SDK** with two new on-device speech models (Nemotron Speech 3.5 Streaming ASR + Qwen3 TTS, both 600M params) plus early RTX Spark support and DLSS 5 with 3D-Guided Neural Rendering for RTX 50 Series. The games-of-note thread surfaces a meaningful pricing-debate counterpoint: **Warhorse co-founder Martin Klima** called base-price increases "long overdue" and framed GTA 6's $80 tier as the industry's best hope for moving the ceiling — a developer-side argument that sits directly against the consumer/retailer backlash the page has tracked. A **new regulatory front** also opened: Italy's AGCM launched an EU-coordinated consumer-protection probe into virtual-currency practices at Activision Blizzard, Riot, Ubisoft and Supercell — distinct from the Stop Killing Games preservation campaign.
