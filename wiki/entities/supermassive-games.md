# Supermassive Games

## Summary

Supermassive Games is a British video game developer known for narrative-driven horror games, most notably *Until Dawn* (2015) and *The Dark Pictures Anthology* series. The studio is currently experiencing severe contraction: **as of August 2026, it has conducted three rounds of layoffs in three years** — 90 employees cut in February 2024, 36 in July 2025, and up to 75 in its August 2026 redundancy consultation [[supermassive-games-begins-third-round-of-layoffs-in-three-years-9bc14ff3]].

The timing of the third layoff wave is particularly pointed: it comes **less than three months after *Directive 8020* shipped in May 2026 to a Mixed/Average Metacritic 72** [[supermassive-games-begins-third-round-of-layoffs-in-three-years-9bc14ff3]]. Directive 8020 had been pitched as the launch title for a second season of The Dark Pictures Anthology, making the immediate post-launch contraction a hard commercial signal — the game meant to anchor the studio's next multi-title arc appears to have commercially underperformed [[supermassive-games-begins-third-round-of-layoffs-in-three-years-9bc14ff3]].

**The studio has also lost its flagship IP.** Sony replaced Supermassive with Firesprite as the developer on *Until Dawn 2*, ending Supermassive's stewardship of the franchise it created [[until-dawn-2-developer-change-supermassive-to-firesprite-720b9386]]. This leaves the studio primarily anchored to The Dark Pictures Anthology at a moment when the anthology's reception is weakening — *Directive 8020* reviewed as "adrift," with critics split on whether its over-the-shoulder camera and Turning Points rewind mechanic represent evolution or execution shortfall [[the-dark-pictures-anthology-has-never-been-more-adrift-directive-8020-review-50a2449d]].

> Supermassive Games has announced it will begin a redundancy consultation process anticipated to result in the loss of up to 75 colleagues, describing it as a necessary step to help ensure the sustainability of the company.

> This is the third time in the last couple of years the British team was forced into redundancies, having laid off 90 people in February 2024 and then another 36 in July 2025.

> The cuts arrived less than three months after the release of Directive 8020, which was eventually released in May 2026 to a generally mixed critical reception, with a Metacritic score of 72.
