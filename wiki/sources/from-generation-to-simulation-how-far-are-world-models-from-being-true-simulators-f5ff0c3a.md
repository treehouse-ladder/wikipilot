## Excerpts

> World models have achieved functional substitution in interaction and controllability for specific scenarios, but remain short of traditional simulators in formal guarantees of physical laws, structured state feedback, and reproducible long-horizon evolution.

> This paper presents a systematic, capability-based study using eight capabilities of a traditional simulator: asset construction, physics engine, interaction, controllability, stability, state feedback, diversity, and evaluation metrics. We trace the evolution of three main technical routes — latent dynamics, video generation, and joint-embedding prediction — and map 200 representative works from 2018 to June 2026 onto the eight capabilities.

> We find that world models have reached or approached simulator-level competence on interaction and controllability in narrow, well-defined domains, but the four hardest capabilities — stable long-horizon rollout, physics-law guarantees, structured state feedback, and reproducible evaluation — remain open problems. The gap is not merely one of scale: it reflects architectural choices that trade interpretability and guarantees for generative expressiveness.
