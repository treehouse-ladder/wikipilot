## Excerpts

> Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney called Steam's requirement that developers disclose AI use in their games "really irresponsible," comparing it to a "Scarlet Letter" that stigmatizes legitimate use of AI tools in game development.

> Sweeney argued that Steam's AI disclosure policy creates an uneven playing field: it singles out AI-generated assets for public labeling while other software tools (physics engines, procedural generators, middleware) require no such disclosure, making AI an exception that invites consumer backlash without equivalent scrutiny of other automation.

> The Epic CEO's position directly conflicts with consumer-protection advocates and developer labor advocates who argue that AI asset disclosure allows buyers to make informed purchasing decisions and protects artists whose work may have been used in AI training without consent.

> Sweeney did not address Epic's own AI policies or whether Epic Games Store would adopt similar disclosure requirements, leaving his public position framed as a critique of Steam's specific implementation rather than a blanket opposition to AI transparency.
